The intelligence layer for last-mile delivery

Every route remembers.

Drivers know things no map does. ParcelVox captures that knowledge by voice, a dispatcher approves it, and it plays back to the next driver at exactly the right stop — so what one driver learns, the next one inherits.

Every pin is something a driver learned the hard way
The problem

The map ends at the curb.

Parcel logistics is highly automated — until the driver leaves the depot. From there, delivery runs on what the driver knows. That knowledge is captured nowhere, so the carrier pays for it three times over.

When a driver hands in their keys, years of route knowledge walk out the door.

? ? ?
What planning sees today · stops, no context

Ramp-up and churn

A new driver needs four to six weeks to reach full speed, and many don’t last that long. The next hire starts from zero.

The same friction, relearned

Every driver rediscovers the entrances, parking spots and doors the last one already found — across thousands of stops.

No insight into why

Dispatch sees that a stop ran late or failed, but never why — so tomorrow’s plan repeats today’s mistakes.

The loop

Speak. Approve. Inherit.

01

The driver says it

“Front bell is broken. Ring at the side door in the yard — code 4711.”

Hands-free, while they work. No typing, no forms — the assistant can even ask what happened after an unusually long stop.

02

AI structures it, a human approves

Access · stop 24 Approved

Structured as access · stop 24, matched to the stop record. A dispatcher gives the final approval in one tap — human in the loop, minimal manual work.

03

The next driver hears it

“Heads up — use the side door in the yard, code 4711.”
Arriving at stop 24

At the right stop, at the right moment. New drivers start with the knowledge of everyone who drove before them.

For drivers

Built with drivers, not against them.

ParcelVox is about what drivers know — never about how fast they drive.

No speed scores

Nothing counts stops per hour or ranks drivers against each other. This is not a performance tool.

About places, not people

Tips describe stops and buildings — a broken bell, a safe parking spot — not the driver.
